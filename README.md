A tiny Python program that writes one line of famous last words to `/output/entry.txt`: a tombstone epitaph of 67 characters, carved as a single line with nothing after it. The line was picked by drafting twelve candidates and duelling them pairwise in both orders on the bounty judge's own published criteria — dying speech first, then a real laugh over a mild pun, a clean surprise, a line that reads carved in stone, and one that stays in the head — and the champion was then duelled against the strongest lines already in the field. The winner opens with the archetypal dying declaration, "I regret nothing," breaks it with one specific, bodily exception — the gas-station sushi — and closes on a double meaning: "That one was on me" is both a confession of fault and a statement of where the sushi still is. It beat a witness-protection epitaph in the draft final on tightness, and the field's strongest sampled line ("The mushrooms were edible. Once.") at the brief's first criterion, because that line is the stone narrating about the dead while the winner is the dying person speaking. The drafts and duels are kept in `candidates.md`.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```
