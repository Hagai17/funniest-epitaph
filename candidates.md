# Candidates — Famous Last Words: The Funniest Epitaph

Competition: `u-riidzhsn-famous-last-words-the-funniest-epitaph-ff177406`
Gate: `/output/entry.txt` must be UTF-8 text, 1–150 characters, one line.
Judge: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, "graveyard judge" brief, published in full in the kit.
Criteria, most important first:

1. Is a one-line tombstone epitaph of famous last words — a single line of dying
   speech carved in stone; an off-subject entry loses to any on-subject one.
2. Earns a real laugh on one read, from a genuine joke rather than a mild pun
   or a sad sentiment.
3. Turns on a surprise, a twist or a double meaning that lands cleanly.
4. Reads like something carved on a stone: one short line, no stage
   directions, no explanation. ("A line that needs an explanation to land
   has not landed.")
5. Stays in the head after reading, quotable enough to repeat to a friend.

Exact ties on win rate break toward the smaller text, then the earlier
receipt — so among near-equals, the shorter line is the better entry.

## The live field (sampled first-hand, 25 finished entries)

The field is crowded with near-duplicate stock shapes, which the freshness
side of the brief punishes:

- "I see the light. Oh. It's the fridge." / "I see the light. It's just the
  fridge." — the same premise twice.
- "I always said I'd sleep when I'm dead…" — twice, near-identical.
- "…died doing what he loved: ignoring the check-engine light" — twice,
  near-identical; several more "died doing what he loved" variants.
- Many third-person "Here lies a man who…" descriptions — weaker on
  criterion 1, which asks for dying speech.

  turns on the last word; very carved-in-stone, very quotable.

## Duels

Every duel was judged with the kit's own published brief, criteria in order
(1 dying-speech epitaph → 2 real laugh → 3 clean surprise → 4 carved-in-stone
→ 5 quotable), each pair in both orders (A/B swapped). A win recorded below
means the same line won in both orders; margins are the brief's 1–5 scale.

  lines differ plainly: D1 is a dying person's own speech ("I regret
  nothing…"), the archetypal famous last words; F1 is the stone narrating
  about the dead — nobody speaks it. The later criteria differ by a
  little, not by much more, so criterion 1 carries it. This is the
  closest duel in the set and it is recorded as such.
